The Problem

A computation gap, not a legal gap

The entitlement is already law. In Re: Inhuman Conditions in 1382 Prisons (23 August 2024) the Supreme Court held s.479 BNSS applies retrospectively to every undertrial, and directed jail superintendents nationwide to process eligible prisoners within three months. Yet in all of 2022, just 537 people were released under the predecessor provision. The law works. The arithmetic never happens.

Why release does not happen

  • The prisoner doesn't know. Two in three undertrials are illiterate or educated below class X.
  • The lawyer can't scale. A DLSA panel advocate with 200 files cannot hand-compute half-terms across multi-charge FIRs.
  • The superintendent has a duty but no instrument. s.479(3) BNSS obliges him to apply — with no tool to identify who qualifies.
  • The committee reviews file by file. Quarterly UTRC sittings crawl through cases one at a time.

What Bail Reckoner changes

  • Computes the exact date eligibility accrues — the ½ rule, the new ⅓ rule, and the full-term cap.
  • Screens an entire jail roster in one pass and ranks prisoners by priority for release.
  • Speaks to the prisoner in Hindi, in plain words, ending with NALSA 15100.
  • Drafts the application under the correct section, with the strongest ground pleaded first.
How It Works

Four inputs. A defensible finding.

Enter the charges and the date of arrest. The engine applies codified statute and binding precedent — then shows its working, line by line.

1 · Charges & provisions

Select from 72 offences mapped across IPC↔BNS with maximum and mandatory-minimum sentence, cognizability, bailability, compounding status and trial forum. The gravest charge governs the category.

2 · Custody clock

Computes served time against the s.479 BNSS threshold — one-half, or one-third for a first-time offender — plus the 60/90/180-day default-bail window under s.187 BNSS, and flags custody exceeding the maximum sentence.

3 · Judicial discretion

Scores the triple test — flight risk, tampering with evidence, influencing witnesses — with itemised reasons and a gravity floor, so a serious charge can never read as low risk.

4 · Statutory bars

Detects UAPA s.43D(5), NDPS s.37, PMLA s.45 and the SC/ST Act s.18 bar — and correctly applies their carve-outs, including the PMLA proviso for a woman or an amount under ₹1 crore.

5 · Precedent applied

Eighteen authorities surfaced contextually — Satender Kumar Antil, Arnesh Kumar, K.A. Najeeb, Mohd Muslim, Moti Ram — matched to the offence category, not pasted in bulk.

6 · Output you can file

A reasoning trace showing every rule that fired, a procedural checklist, suggested bail conditions, and a draft application under the correct section — printable as a PDF.

Special logic that fires

  • UAPA extends the default-bail window to 180 days
  • NDPS applies the s.37 bar only at commercial quantity
  • PMLA auto-detects the s.45 proviso (woman / under-16 / infirm / under ₹1 crore)
  • SC/ST s.18 correctly limited to anticipatory bail, so regular bail is not wrongly barred
  • s.479 multiple-case bar and the death/life exclusion both enforced

Rules, not predictions

We deliberately did not build a model that scores who "deserves" bail. Training on historical bail orders launders caste, class and religious bias into an algorithm, and no court should defer to a black box on personal liberty.

Every output is derived from codified statute and binding precedent, and ships with a trace showing the rule and the fact that fired it.

Questions

Frequently asked

Does this replace the judge?

No, and it is designed not to. The tool never states whether bail should be granted. It computes statutory entitlement, structures the discretionary factors the court must consider, and produces a record to support a speaking order. Discretion under ss. 480–483 BNSS remains entirely with the court.

Why not machine learning?

Because the training data would be historical bail orders, which encode the very biases that put a disproportionate number of SC, ST, OBC and Muslim undertrials behind bars. An opaque score on personal liberty would also fail judicial scrutiny. A transparent rules engine can be audited, corrected and defended in open court.

Is the legal database verified?

The 72-offence knowledge base is a substantial, carefully assembled reference set, but it is not an exhaustive codification of the statute book. It must be verified section-by-section by a qualified legal reviewer before any live deployment. The tool states this on every screen, and the demonstration roster is synthetic data.

Does it handle the new criminal laws?

Yes. Every offence carries both its IPC section and its BNS 2023 equivalent, and the procedural provisions are cited in both CrPC and BNSS form. The engine implements the BNSS-specific changes: the one-third threshold for first-time offenders, the express bar where cases are pending in more than one offence, and the exclusion of life-imprisonment offences from s.479.

What about privacy?

Nothing is transmitted. The tool is a single HTML file with no network calls, no analytics and no storage — every computation happens in the browser and is discarded when the tab closes. This is deliberate: prisoner data should never leave the institution.

Which languages are supported?

English and Hindi across the entire interface, including generated results, offence names, statutory notes and judgment summaries. Case citations and statute short titles remain in Roman script, as is standard Indian court practice. Bhashini API integration for all 22 scheduled languages is on the roadmap.
